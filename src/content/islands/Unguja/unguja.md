Unguja is the largest and most populous island in the Zanzibar Archipelago. It is commonly referred to simply as "Zanzibar Island." It sits in the Indian Ocean off the coast of mainland Tanzania. The island is world-famous for its white-sand beaches, historic architecture, and vibrant Swahili culture.

Key Attractions

• Stone Town: The island's historic and cultural capital. It features coral-stone buildings, narrow alleys, and grand Arab palaces [Zanzibar].
• Jozani Forest: The island's only national park. It protects a rare groundwater forest that is home to the endangered Red Colobus monkey.
• Northern Beaches: The coastal villages of Nungwi and Kendwa [Zanzibar]. They offer turquoise waters, luxury resorts, and a lively nightlife scene [Zanzibar].
• Spice Plantations: Agricultural farms in the rural interior. They grow aromatic crops like cloves, vanilla, cardamom, and nutmeg.

Economy & Travel

• Tourism & Trade: The island's economy relies heavily on beach resorts, spice exports, and marine fishing.
• Transport: Travelers arrive via international flights to Abeid Amani Karume International Airport or via high-speed ferries from Dar es Salaam [Dar es Salaam].
