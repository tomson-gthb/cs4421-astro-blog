 Disposable / RFID

Points:

    Very small, very low power.

    Externally powered: only works in presence of an electrical field.

    Fixed function: emits an ID string, typically 128-bit.

    Uses: ID cards, hotel room keys, inventory tracking.

    NFC: similar idea, built into smartphones or other devices.

Concepts:

    RFID (Radio-Frequency Identification):

        A tiny chip + antenna.

        No battery in many cases; powered by the reader’s radio field.

        When in range of a reader, it sends back a unique ID.

    Fixed function:

        Not a general-purpose computer; it does one job: respond with an ID.

        Very limited logic, minimal circuitry.

    128-bit ID:

        Enough unique IDs for enormous numbers of tags.

        Used to identify objects, not to run programs.

    NFC (Near Field Communication):

        A short-range version of RFID, used in phones for contactless payments, transit cards, pairing devices.

        Your phone can act as an RFID/NFC tag or reader.

These are the simplest “computers” in the zoo: almost no computation, just identification.
