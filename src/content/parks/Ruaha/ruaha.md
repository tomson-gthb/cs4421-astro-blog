Ruaha National Park is Tanzania's largest and wildest national park. It covers a massive 20,226 square kilometers of protected wilderness in the country's southern safari circuit. It is famous for its completely raw atmosphere, lack of tourist crowds, and landscape filled with ancient baobab trees.

Key Features

• The Great Ruaha River: The park's main lifeblood. It flows along the southeastern border and draws thousands of animals during the dry season.
• Predator Sanctuary: Ruaha holds roughly 10% of the world's wild lion population. It is home to massive prides, leopards, cheetahs, and endangered African wild dogs.
• Giant Elephant Herds: The park protects one of East Africa's largest elephant populations, numbering around 10,000 individuals.
• Ecological Crossroads: It is a unique transition zone where East African savanna meets Southern African miombo woodlands. This allows visitors to spot rare roan and sable antelopes alongside greater kudu.

Economy & Travel

• Exclusivity: Unlike the crowded northern parks like the Serengeti, Ruaha sees a very low volume of visitors. This offers an incredibly private safari experience.
• Transport: The park is located about 130 kilometers west of Iringa town. Travelers can drive in or take light aircraft flights directly to the Msembe or Jongomeru airstrips
