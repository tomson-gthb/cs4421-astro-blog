Kitulo National Park is a protected botanical reserve in southwestern Tanzania. It sits on a high-altitude plateau surrounded by the Kipengere Range mountains [Mbeya]. Locals proudly call it "Bustani ya Mungu" (God's Garden) [Mbeya], while international botanists celebrate it as the "Serengeti of Flowers."

Key Features

• Rare Wildflowers: The park protects 350 species of vascular plants. This includes 45 distinct species of terrestrial orchids and massive fields of yellow, orange, and purple blooms.
• Peak Season: The absolute best time to visit is during the rainy season from November to April, when the plateau explodes into a vibrant, multi-coloured carpet of flowers.
• Unique Fauna: The park protects critical alpine grasslands. It serves as a refuge for the rare Kipunji monkey, montane birds, and breeding colonies of Blue Swallows.

Hiking & Travel

• Activities: The park is primarily designed for walking safaris, hiking, and bird watching. It offers scenic day-treks across open, rolling green meadows.
• Access: The park is located roughly 100 kilometres from the city of Mbeya [Mbeya]. Visitors typically access the park by taking a 4x4 vehicle up the steep, winding roads via Chimala town.
